## Argument

- The product deserves close inspection: a directly shoppable product stage shows its form, name and demo price.
- Different needs call for different objects: a varied selection provides category and catalog entry points.
- Details change a buying decision: image-led product storytelling leads to specifications, without fabricating performance claims.
- Shopping remains simple: direct access to product, cart and simulated checkout remains visible.
- This is a template: the same product-selection grammar must accommodate a cosmetic bottle without an industrial costume.

## Seven grounded candidates, ordered by resonance

1. Campaign studio — a commercial still-life set; the featured product dominates an unbroken photographic field and immediate purchase controls anchor the scene. Product clarity is strong; familiar product-launch composition is the honest risk.
2. Photographer's contact sheet — image crops and full views become a variable-scale selection surface; its active image is enlarged without concealing the shopping controls. Risk: the page may feel like a portfolio rather than a store.
3. Selection table — a product editor's arrangement of objects, each isolated on flat brand-colored surfaces, gathered into a shallow arc; selecting an object brings its image, name, price and purchase action forward. This is the assigned direction, not a workshop-table simulation. Risk: an overfull arc could compromise clarity on phones.
4. Material library — tactile product details become paired large samples and compact buying facts; a sample can be exchanged without changing the information hierarchy. Risk: cosmetic suitability is stronger than broad hardware suitability.
5. Department-store window — a deep full-color display with a few monumental objects and unobtrusive direct shopping controls. Risk: set decoration could compete with the product.
6. Folding selection folio — category chapters open into product spreads with a stable shopping edge. Risk: the fold metaphor can obscure ordinary browsing.
7. Sequential product study — one object shifts from overall view to material detail while purchase information remains stationary. Risk: too much dwell time before the catalog.

## Challenger verdicts, before borrowing

- Centre-rail reference setting: declined on audience identification and product clarity. A compact scholarly reading block does not help this store's visual product discovery. Keep disciplined alignment and a stable reading order, not hairlines, rail keys or apparatus.
- Pulp rocketship plate: declined on both axes. A rocket-flight navigation metaphor and porthole controls do not explain shopping. Keep decisive photographic scale and commitment to the red/navy fields, not retro illustration.
- Tensegrity breathing column: declined on both axes. Force tracing is not a buying task and overfits hardware. Keep one coordinated motion system with stable resting states, not a force graph or annotations.
- Chromatophore skin: declined on both axes. Cellular signals and context camouflage obscure the merchandise and are not native to the audience's shopping scene. Keep coordinated active-state change, not shaders or living skin.
- Data-sublime field: declined on both axes. Barcode strobing and color elimination conflict with brand and product inspection. Keep legible active-item hierarchy and an immediate motion stop, not data costume or strobing.
- Rhythm-machine step row: declined on both axes. Sequencing audio is unrelated to choosing products and would introduce unexplained controls. Keep immediate selected-state feedback and a steady interaction rhythm, not LEDs or audio playback.

## Direction raises

- Alignment: product name, price and purchase action retain a single stable reading edge as the object changes.
- Scale: the object, not decorative chrome, owns the first viewport.
- Motion: the selection arc and supporting entrances share one coordinated system with reduced-motion stills.
- State: the active object and its purchase information update together; no independently drifting decorations.
- Control: shopping remains readable without motion and operable by keyboard and touch.
